Private Estimation

Auditing failed estimators to design mechanisms

From auditing to mechanisms

  • Running problem: estimate a typical income from a private sample.
  • Tempting estimators often work on ordinary data.
  • Differential privacy is a worst-case neighboring-dataset claim.

Question: How do we turn a failed estimator into a valid mechanism without losing the statistical goal?

Today’s contract

  • Dataset size: \(n=1{,}000\).
  • Adjacency: substitute one row.
  • Total budget per released estimate: $ arepsilon=1$ and the course value of \(delta\).
  • Accuracy target: sample or population mean, except when we deliberately switch to a median.
  • Data example: Fulton PUMS income.

Every proposal must state its target, public inputs, private computations, and released output.

The loop we will repeat

  1. Propose the estimator we wish were safe.
  2. Audit it on a chosen neighboring pair.
  3. Diagnose the exact data-dependent step.
  4. Repair that step with a mechanism and account for its budget.

Question: Which step protects us from mistaking “stable on my sample” for privacy?

Public-menu contract

  • Grids, bin edges, candidate thresholds, and transforms are fixed before private data is touched.
  • Public data may choose the menu.
  • Private data must privately choose from the menu.

Post-processing protects later computation only after every private choice has already been made by a DP mechanism.

Proposal 1 — bounded-range mean

Assume every value is publicly known to lie in \([L,U]\) and release

\[\bar x+\mathcal N(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{DP}}^2), \qquad \Delta_2(\bar x)=\frac{U-L}{n}.\]

This is a valid template when the range and Gaussian calibration are public.

Sensitivity sets the noise scale

  • A conservative salary domain can be far wider than the observed data.
  • Here the public width is \(10^8\) dollars.
  • At \(n=1{,}000\), the calibrated noise standard deviation is about 187,788 dollars.

The mechanism may be private and still be useless for a mean near tens of thousands of dollars.

Valid—but useful?

Question: Did privacy fail here, or did the public sensitivity bound destroy utility?

Diagnosis 1 — bound first

  • Adding noise is not the design step; bounding contribution is.
  • A huge public range makes the raw mean private at huge cost.
  • A narrow range would help—but learning it from private data is itself a private computation.

The next proposals try to discover a useful clipping range.

Proposal 2 — empirical \(\mu\pm4\sigma\)

  1. Compute the sample mean \(\hat\mu\) and standard deviation \(\hat\sigma\).
  2. Clip each row to \([\hat\mu-4\hat\sigma,\hat\mu+4\hat\sigma]\).
  3. Add noise calibrated to the learned width.

A typical audit may look fine

This finite audit does not cross the claimed \(\varepsilon=1\) bound on its chosen pair.

Why the audit is one-sided

  • Finding a separating witness disproves a privacy claim.
  • Failing to find one does not quantify over all neighboring datasets and events.
  • Here both the clipping interval and the output noise scale depend on private data.

Question: Can an empirical audit ever certify the universal DP definition?

Diagnosis 2 — preprocessing is part of the release

The mechanism is not

\[\text{fixed clipping}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{DP mean}.\]

It is

\[D\longrightarrow(\hat\mu(D),\hat\sigma(D)) \longrightarrow\text{data-dependent clipping and noise}.\]

Those first arrows need privacy accounting too.

Proposal 3 — empirical quantile clipping

  • Learn the 1st and 99th percentiles from the private sample.
  • Clip to those empirical thresholds.
  • Release a noisy clipped mean.

Quantiles are robust on typical data, but the learned order statistics can jump across a constructed gap.

A witness catches the leak

Question: Why can the output laws separate even though the final mean is clipped?

Diagnosis 3 — post-processing starts too late

  • The lower and upper thresholds are data-dependent.
  • They change both the deterministic clipped mean and its noise scale.
  • Noise on the final number does not privatize thresholds that were learned non-privately.

The repair must privatize the bounds before using them.

Repair 1 — private quantile-clipped mean

Select public threshold candidates with private rank scores, then clip and release the mean.

Composition is a ledger

The total is \(\varepsilon=1\) by sequential composition.

Reallocate without changing the guarantee

Move the bounds’ share; the slices still sum to \(\varepsilon=1\). The proxy is bookkeeping, not measured error—choose an allocation with an accuracy experiment.

A different target — the median

The median resists an isolated outlier on ordinary samples, so it is tempting to release

\[\operatorname{median}(D)+\text{value noise}.\]

But robustness describes typical contamination; DP sensitivity asks for the largest change over all neighboring datasets.

Robust is not worst-case stable

Repair 2 — change the error metric

The rank utility

\[u(D,t)=-\left|\#\{x_i\le t\}-\frac n2\right|\]

changes by at most one under substitution, independent of dollar gaps.

Inverse-sensitivity landscape

Question: Why is spending privacy on rank error more useful than adding noise in dollars?

From clipping to localization

For a roughly Gaussian population, useful clipping needs private estimates of both scale and location.

  • Estimating \(\sigma\) from raw values is entangled with unknown \(\mu\).
  • Estimating \(\mu\) needs a grid whose resolution matches the unknown scale.
  • Each private choice must appear in the budget ledger.

Localize scale without the mean

Disjoint pairwise differences \(|X_i-X_j|\) cancel the mean; a noisy histogram privately selects a scale bin.

Localize the center on a public grid

The private scale determines a useful public-grid resolution; noisy counts then localize the center before the final clipped mean.

Method map

Proposal Privacy status Main lesson
Public-range noisy mean valid, poor utility sensitivity can dominate
Empirical \(\mu\pm k\sigma\) clipping invalid private preprocessing needs accounting
Empirical quantile clipping invalid robust thresholds are still private
Private quantile-clipped mean valid spend budget on bounds and mean
Median value noise valid, poor utility value sensitivity ignores robustness
Rank-based median valid change the metric, not just the noise
Gaussian localization valid; model-dependent accuracy privacy and accuracy are separate claims

Takeaways and bridge

  • State the target, public menu, private choices, and released output.
  • Use audits to find counterexamples—not to certify privacy.
  • Diagnose the data-dependent step before choosing its repair.
  • Account for every private selection by composition.
  • Validate statistical assumptions separately from privacy.
