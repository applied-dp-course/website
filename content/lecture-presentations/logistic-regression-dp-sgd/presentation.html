Bernoulli GLM

\[p(y\mid\theta,\phi)=\exp\{(y\theta-b(\theta))/a(\phi)+c(y,\phi)\}\]

\[\eta_i=w^Tx_i+b,\quad \mu_i=E[Y_i\mid x_i],\quad \operatorname{logit}(\mu_i)=\eta_i\]

For Bernoulli data: \(p_i=\sigma(\eta_i)\) and \(\ell_i=-y_i\log p_i-(1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)\).

Full-batch GD first

\[L(w)=n^{-1}\sum_i\ell_i(w),\qquad w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta_{GD}\nabla L(w_t)\]

GD and SGD

\[L(w)=n^{-1}\sum_i\ell_i(w),\qquad w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\nabla L(w_t)\]

\[w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta |B_t|^{-1}\sum_{i\in B_t}\nabla\ell_i(w_t)\]

An SGD batch is random, hence its gradient is random. On 2-vs-3 below, GD uses \(\eta=0.06\) and SGD uses batch 64 with \(\eta=0.15\); both make six full passes. GD has one epoch-end update per pass; the SGD curve retains every mini-batch update, positioned by fractional completed epoch.

A gradient is an additive query

With the intercept included in \(\tilde x_i\):

\[g_i(w_t)=(p_i-y_i)\tilde x_i,\qquad \sum_{i\in B_t}g_i(w_t).\]

Conditional on current \(w_t\), the sum is additive over records. Adaptive composition handles later queries chosen from earlier private outputs.

Exact Poisson DP-SGD

  1. Include each row independently with probability \(q\).
  2. Clip \(g_i\) to \(\bar g_i=g_i\min(1,C/\|g_i\|)\).
  3. Form \((\sum_i\bar g_i+N(0,(\sigma C)^2I))/(qN_{ref})\).
  4. Apply the update, including an empty noise-only batch.

The public regularizer \(\lambda w\) is applied after the data gradient and changes no sensitivity.

One-step Poisson-inclusion ROC

This is a one-step witness, not a DP-SGD ROC or composed bound. At the honest course display value \(\sigma=2\), the record-present and record-absent laws are close—that is the privacy gain.

Privacy profile

\[\delta_M(\varepsilon)=\max\{D_{e^\varepsilon}(P\Vert Q),D_{e^\varepsilon}(Q\Vert P)\}\]

Privacy-loss distribution

\[L(O)=\log(p(O)/q(O)),\quad O\sim P\]

At threshold \(\varepsilon\), the privacy profile is the difference between two tail bounds:

\[\delta(\varepsilon)=P[L>\varepsilon]-e^{\varepsilon}Q[L>\varepsilon].\]

Where sampling accounting separates

The source comparison fixes a unit-sensitivity Gaussian mechanism, \(\delta=10^{-6}\), \(t=10^4\), \(k=1\), and Poisson rate \(q=k/t=10^{-4}\). Every line uses the original numerical artifact on one common \(\sigma\) grid; the review screenshot is not the data source.

Random allocation is a bridge, not exact shuffle accounting

  • The shuffle lower and upper bounds leave a wide unresolved interval.
  • Poisson PLD accounting can therefore look dramatically better than the shuffle upper bound.
  • Random allocation keeps exactly \(k\) participations per record, while its FS25 and PLD upper bounds approach the Poisson curve in this regime.

Sources: Feldman–Shenfeld, Privacy amplification by random allocation (NeurIPS 2025), and Efficient privacy loss accounting for subsampling and random allocation, arXiv:2602.17284v2.
